# Pause: Pilot, "Barcelona"

**Lesson:** the grid gives a site its structure, and Gaudí-style detail gives it personality. A good site needs both.
**Runtime:** about 1:45, vertical 1080x1920, no dialogue (captions + music + sound effects, Alan Becker style).

## The series in one paragraph
Kolurin and Kuwolskii live in a pencil-sketch cartoon universe: their studio, the main base. When a client's website is stuck, the Pause button starts to glow (our magic key) and pulls them into another reality. There they fully exist in that world's look and physics, and they pick up a design lesson along the way. The button pulls them home, and they use what they learnt on the client's site.

## Cast
- **Kolurin**: locs, button-up, hands in pockets, deadpan, unimpressed by almost everything. Does the physical work (kicks things into place). Very hard to impress, so the moment he finally is impressed matters.
- **Kuwolskii**: cap, goatee, hoodie, cargo pants. Playful, always first to touch things. Signature move: the finger gun with "///" lines. His "pew" sets off the magic.

## Look rules
- **Base (cartoon universe):** pencil on cream sketchbook paper, wobbly lines, monochrome with one accent colour (Pause red).
- **Other realities:** the characters stay drawn, but they take on that world's light. In Barcelona: warm Mediterranean colour, real cast shadows on the pavement, motion blur, film grain to match the footage. The background is **real video**, with the cartoon composited on top (like the reference clip).
- **The crossover moments** (base → reality → base) are always the Pause button: paper tears open onto real footage, and real footage crumples back into paper.

## Footage needed (Barcelona plates)
Shoot **vertical 9:16, 4K, 24 or 30 fps**. Use a tripod or very slow, smooth moves; locked-off shots are easiest to put the characters into. Get **10–15 s of each**, plus one still photo per shot. Leave empty space in the **lower-middle** of the frame for the characters.

1. Passeig de Gràcia pavement with the hexagonal tiles, low angle, people passing in the background.
2. An Eixample corner (the cut-off corner of a block) with a street-name sign visible.
3. A high view over the Eixample grid (Bunkers del Carmel, or any rooftop).
4. Casa Batlló or La Pedrera facade, looking up.
5. Café terrace table; churros + chocolate if possible; pigeons are a bonus.
6. Golden hour city view (for the exit).
7. Room tone/street ambience audio from 2–3 of these spots.

If filming there isn't possible, the alternatives are licensed stock footage or AI-generated photoreal plates.
